First things first

Meet
Siesta Key.

Siesta Key is a barrier island off the coast of Sarasota, home to powdery white quartz sand that stays cool under your feet—even in the Florida sun.

The island has three distinct areas: Siesta Key Village on the north end for shops, restaurants and nightlife; the residential mid-Key; and the quieter Turtle Beach area to the south.

Two bridges connect the Key to the mainland. It is easy to reach, but traffic and parking get difficult during peak season. That is where Siesta Free Ride makes the day easier.

99% pure quartz crystal sand, free public access, lifeguards, concessions, volleyball, playgrounds and picnic pavilions. Open 8am to sunset.

The south-end gem

Turtle
Beach

Excellent shelling, fewer crowds, a boat ramp, picnic tables and nearby camping. Coarser sand, with a relaxed and local feel.

Public beaches do not allow dogs, except service animals.
03

Room for the family

Less crowded and close to Point of Rocks, one of the Gulf Coast’s favorite snorkeling spots. There are no lifeguards here.

Swim at your own risk.

Walk, browse, repeat

Siesta Key
Village.

A walkable strip of boutiques, surf shops, art galleries, restaurants and souvenir stores near the north bridge—the social center of the island.

Look for local art and photography, handmade jewelry, resort wear, surf gear and Florida-made gifts. Several shops carry locally made hot sauces, candles and crafts.
